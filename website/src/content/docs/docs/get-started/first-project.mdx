This page takes you from an installed Commitarium to your first merged change on your own code. It assumes you have [installed it](/docs/get-started/install/) and [added your agents](/docs/get-started/agents/): a Claude and a Codex agent is best, so the reviewer comes from another provider than the lead.

## 1. Turn on Developer mode

Open **Settings**, and in **General** tick **Developer mode**. The window becomes the workshop: your projects and their chats on the left, the chat with Mayka in the middle, and cards for the plan, work orders, agents, preview and sync on the right. More in [Turning it on](/docs/developer-mode/turning-it-on/).

## 2. Bring in your repository

Click **All** beside **Projects**, then **Import project…**, and choose the folder of a clean Git repository. On a server, you give its https address instead, and a token for a private one. Commitarium copies it into its own private Git server; your folder is never changed. More in [Importing a project](/docs/your-own-code/importing/).

## 3. Read Mayka's first look

Mayka reads the code by herself and writes a first look: what the project is, whether its stack is right, and a numbered list of what could come next. Your project starts in **Assists me**: she proposes, you approve. More in [Mayka's first look](/docs/your-own-code/first-look/).

<Screenshot id="first-look" alt="Mayka's first look at an imported project, with a numbered list of suggestions and the Build for me card" />

## 4. Ask for your first change

Tell Mayka what you want, as you would a colleague: "do 1 and 2 from your list", or "add rate limiting to the login endpoint". She writes it up as a **work order** and picks the agents and models; you approve the card. You can also create one yourself with **+ New work order**.

A work order then goes through four steps, each recorded:

1. **Clarify:** the goal is settled, with a brief for the agents.
2. **Plan:** the lead proposes a plan; the reviewer challenges it until they agree.
3. **Implement:** the lead builds it in commit-sized steps; the reviewer checks the code, runs the tests and requests changes where needed.
4. **Merge:** once the checks pass, it merges into main, by you or by the project's merge policy.

<Screenshot id="work-order" alt="A finished work order: its four steps, the merged pull request and what it cost" />

More in [Work orders](/docs/developer-mode/work-orders/), and for something bigger, think it through first with [plan mode](/docs/developer-mode/plan-mode/).

## 5. Get the result into your repository

Nothing reaches your own repository until you say so. The **Sync** card shows the merged work orders waiting; **Pull** brings them into your folder as commits, and pushing them to GitHub or elsewhere stays yours to do. More in [Syncing with your repository](/docs/your-own-code/syncing/).

## Choose how much runs on its own

- **Assists me** (the default for imported projects): Mayka proposes and you approve each work order, plan and merge, if the project's settings say so.
- **Builds everything for me**: Mayka owns the project and only asks when a decision needs you. See [Assists me or Builds everything](/docs/apps/assists-or-builds/) and [Project settings](/docs/developer-mode/project-settings/).
