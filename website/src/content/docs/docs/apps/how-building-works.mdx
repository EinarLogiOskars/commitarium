This page explains what happens between your sentence and a working app, so you know what you are looking at.

## The vision

Every app starts with a **vision**: a short statement of why the app exists, who it is for, and what it should become, followed by a plan of the work that gets it there. Mayka writes it from what you told her, and lists anything she had to guess.

<Screenshot id="vision" alt="A project's vision page: the statement at the top and the prioritised plan below" />

## Priorities

The plan is split into priorities:

- **P0** is what the app needs first to be useful.
- **P1** is what comes next, and so on.

Each item has a label such as `P0.2`, a short title, and a sentence about the outcome. Big items hold smaller ones. Each smallest item becomes one **work order**.

## Work orders

A work order is one focused piece of work that an agent builds in one go. Most go through these steps:

1. **Clarify**: what exactly is wanted, written as a brief.
2. **Plan**: the lead agent writes a plan, commit by commit. The reviewer challenges it until they agree.
3. **Implement**: the lead writes the code.
4. **Review**: the reviewer checks the code, and the lead makes corrections.
5. **Merge**: the change joins the app's main code.

Small changes can skip steps. [Work orders in Developer mode](/docs/developer-mode/work-orders/) goes into the detail.

## Review

The lead and the reviewer are separate sessions, ideally on different AI providers, so no model signs off its own work. Every change goes through a pull request in Commitarium's private Git server, where the whole history is kept.

## Merge

Before a change merges, it is brought up to date with everything merged since it started. Whether it then merges by itself or waits for you depends on the project's [merge policy](/docs/developer-mode/project-settings/). Apps made with New app merge by themselves.

After a merge, the app is rebuilt from the new code and its window reloads. Commitarium checks that the app actually starts before calling it ready. If it doesn't, the lead gets the evidence and up to three tries to fix it.

## Autopilot

**Autopilot** is Mayka driving the work for you. Given a goal, such as "finish P0", she creates the work orders, picks the agents and models, starts them, and sees them through to merged, two side by side when that helps.

Autopilot recovers on its own:

- an interrupted turn is retried;
- approved work is brought up to date before it merges;
- a failure pauses a work order instead of ending it, so nothing is lost.

When Mayka can't fix something, she tells you in plain words, with one thing to do.
